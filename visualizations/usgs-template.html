Inside the USGS method

USGS estimates regular daily variation from selected quiet means, fits a spline, then uses statistical tests to decide which amplitude should determine K. Explore actual calculations from Kiruna, December 2023.

Monthly calibration→Select quiet means→Regression intersections→Cubic spline→Amplitude tests → digital K

1. Calibrate quiet-mean limits from the month

The monthly distribution of mean values supplies upper and lower selection limits. Within-block ranges supply a separate disturbance limit. The ten quiet dates are ranked using H.

2. Keep quiet means; reject disturbed blocks

X/Y are converted to H/D, centered, and quantized to the reference units. Means are rejected for excessive range, out-of-limit level, or insufficient valid samples.

3. Fit line segments, then a cubic spline

Sliding groups of three accepted means define regression segments. Their intersections become spline knots. Accepted means from neighboring days support the fit near midnight.

Piecewise-linear fit / intersectionsCubic-spline regular curve

Support points can lie outside the displayed 00–24 UT window. When accepted means are sparse, the routine uses its daily mean/mode fallback and fills long spline-knot intervals.

4. Subtract the curve, then test the amplitude

Residual = observation − spline. USGS can keep the residual range, choose the unfiltered range, or reduce the amplitude for a statistically significant trend. The final amplitude need not equal the residual range.

5. Convert amplitudes to decimal digital K

The final amplitudes are rounded to integer nT, then converted using the USGS digital-K relation and station K9. Combined K keeps the larger H/D value. The calculator also reports truncated integer K.
